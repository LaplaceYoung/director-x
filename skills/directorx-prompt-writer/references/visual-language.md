# Visual language

## Describe evidence, not intention

Translate abstract intent into visible evidence.

- Weak: luxurious, emotional, cinematic
- Better: brushed aluminum edge catches a narrow side light; restrained charcoal palette; shallow depth of field; slow controlled hand movement

Do not tell an image model about motives, backstory, off-screen events, editing, or sound. Do not tell a video model to render simultaneous actions that cannot physically fit in the shot.

## Build the frame in layers

1. subject identity and state
2. visible action
3. environment
4. framing and camera position
5. lens character and depth
6. key, fill, rim, practical, and ambient light
7. dominant, secondary, and accent color relationship
8. material and surface response
9. atmosphere and environmental motion
10. exact in-frame text, only when necessary

## Continuity locks

Lock only stable production facts:

- facial structure, hairstyle, body proportions
- wardrobe silhouette, material, color, wear state
- product geometry, labels, ports, controls, finish
- recurring props and their condition
- scene layout and light direction
- dominant palette

Do not lock pose, expression, framing, or camera movement globally. Those belong to each shot.

## Character and product references

For recurring characters, prefer a clean identity reference plus a full-body wardrobe reference. For products, prefer front, rear, side, detail, and in-use views when available. State which reference controls which property.

## Style references

Extract transferable properties rather than requesting a copy:

- composition grammar
- contrast and exposure
- palette distribution
- lens and depth behavior
- texture
- camera energy
- edit rhythm
- typography role

Avoid copying protected characters, logos, footage, dialogue, or distinctive shot sequences.
